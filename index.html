CoCreate: a trust-first marketplace for micro-creator collaborations

CoCreate helps brands and micro-creators discover each other and agree on clear collaboration terms through structured briefs, two-sided trust profiles and rate benchmarks.

Role
Product discovery, customer research, MVP scoping, prototyping, success metrics, strategy
Team
Five members
Context
Product concept for a Software Product Management project at ISB

Problem

Micro-creator collaborations often happen over Instagram DMs and WhatsApp, leaving deal terms scattered across conversations. Brands struggle to assess creator reliability, while creators face ambiguity around deliverables, usage rights, budgets and payment terms.

In interviews with 26 participants, 17 raised scope or usage-rights concerns and 16 raised trust concerns. These issues create negotiation friction and hesitation to commit.

Process

  • Research and discovery: Interviewed 26 creators, brands and agencies on pain points, current workflows and willingness to pay.
  • Prioritisation: Focused on scope ambiguity and counterparty trust, since both directly decide whether a collaboration proceeds.
  • Prototype testing: Tested with two creators and one brand manager to check comprehension and feature usefulness.
  • MVP design: Proposed a concierge-led pilot with 3–5 brands and 30–50 creators to validate real behaviour before investing in automation and payments.
  • Success measurement: Defined Weekly Locked Collaborations as the North Star, with guardrails for disputes, off-platform drop-offs and time to lock.

Decisions

  • Locked Brief: Record deliverables, budget, timeline, usage type and rights duration before work begins.
  • Two-sided Trust Profile: Show reliability signals for brands and creators before commitment.
  • Rate Benchmark: Show 25th percentile, median and 75th percentile rates for comparable creators.
  • Concierge MVP: Manual verification and a limited pilot first, then scale.

Deferred: Escrow, automated matching, agency tools and subscriptions, until core behaviour was validated. I also avoided penalising users for declining opportunities, which could encourage unwanted commitments.

Outcome

17 of 26interviews raised scope or rights concerns
16 of 26interviews raised trust concerns

In a small prototype test, all three participants used the Trust Panel as an initial filter, and the Locked Brief passed the comprehension test. The rate benchmark was only partially validated. The staged roadmap moves from behaviour validation (MVP1) to escrow-backed transactions (MVP2) and agency tools (MVP3). The concierge pilot ran with 4–5 creators and 2–3 brand managers.

Trip Guardian by MMT: an AI-first, collaborative travel platform concept

An AI-integrated group travel planner for MakeMyTrip. AI appears as transparent recommendations rather than a chatbot, with group decision-making and cost-splitting built in, and MMT’s booking record kept as the core.

Role
Sole strategist and designer: research, personas, journey mapping, prioritisation, solution design
Team
Two (merged with a teammate’s technical draft)
Context
Case competition on MakeMyTrip’s AI strategy, built from public information

Problem

AI tools are pulling trip inspiration and planning off-platform, which risks turning MMT into a commoditised checkout layer. Yet the hardest moments, including refunds, disruptions, group cost-splitting and trust in recommendations, remain unsolved. Generic AI can inspire but cannot book, protect or manage a trip, because it owns neither inventory nor payments. That gap is MMT’s clearest advantage, but nothing makes it felt across the journey.

Process

  • Research: Synthesised public data on MMT’s Myra usage and cross-referenced Indian traveller surveys, separating stated AI interest (roughly 60–70%) from actual trust in autonomous booking (roughly 12–15%).
  • Discovery: Built five personas with deliberately conflicting needs, such as spontaneity versus protection, to stress-test one product.
  • Prioritisation: Scored features on impact, differentiation and feasibility to separate must-build from later-phase work.
  • Tradeoffs: Merged a lean MVP approach with a fuller technical spec into a tiered P0/P1/P2 scope, and kept group features in the MVP given their estimated 30–40% share of bookings.

Decisions

Chose

  • A persistent Trip Object as the core record, spanning inspiration, disruption and memory.
  • AI shown as labelled, dismissible reasoning panels inside structured screens. The chatbot is an optional side channel.
  • Group voting, preference synthesis and cost-splitting in the first release.
  • Trust features such as plain-language cancellation terms and approval before any AI-initiated spend.

Rejected

  • A fully autonomous booking agent, which exceeded the trust ceiling found in research.
  • An AI-only entry point replacing the familiar booking flow.
  • One AI experience for all travellers, since persona needs conflicted.
  • A production-grade backend for the prototype, in favour of a reliable mock-state demo.

Outcome

A strategy and design exercise, so there are no live metrics. Deliverables included a segmentation-to-solution narrative, four frameworks (journey map, architecture, wireframes, priority matrix), a build-ready prototype prompt with a design system, and mock data for 21 destinations.

Key lesson: The most valuable move was deciding where AI should not be the interface. Reversing the default “AI as chatbot” assumption, based on the trust-gap data, shaped the rest of the product.

Money Desk | Razorpay

Turning payment uncertainty into actionable cash-flow decisions for small merchants.

Role
Product discovery, strategy, UX journey design and AI-assisted prototyping
Team
Individual-led, with AI tools supporting research synthesis and implementation
Context
Mobile-first merchant cash-flow and payment-resolution concept for the Razorpay ecosystem

Problem

Small merchants receive payments in staggered amounts but face fixed costs such as wages and supplier advances. Existing workflows show transaction statuses but do not bring together expected cash, overdue collections, in-doubt payments and held settlements into clear next actions. The result is uncertainty about usable cash, time spent chasing buyers and slow resolution of payment issues.

Process

  • Research: Conversations with merchants about payment pain points and existing workarounds.
  • Discovery: Synthesised findings into three core uncertainties: money that is late, money whose status is unclear, and money that is held.
  • Scope: Focused on the cash-flow and resolution workflow rather than a full merchant-management suite.
  • Tradeoffs: Chose actionable guidance over false certainty, merchant control over automation and trust safeguards over feature breadth.

Decisions

  • Cash outlook: Show Certain, Likely and At Risk amounts rather than a single promised forecast.
  • Seller-controlled collections: WhatsApp nudges sent from the merchant’s own account, with frequency limits and no autonomous buyer messaging.
  • Duplicate-payment prevention: Block repeat requests while a transaction is in doubt.
  • Held settlements: Show a reason category, an evidence checklist, status tracking and an escalation path, with release decisions kept human-led.
  • AI boundaries: AI assists with failure classification, message drafting and evidence checks, but never releases holds or moves funds.
  • Rejected: AI repeatedly favoured a COD-to-payment-link feature as the central proposition. I rejected it because it would make Money Desk a payment-feature bundle instead of a product built around cash visibility and resolving uncertainty.

Outcome

An interactive prototype demonstrating the core Identify, Diagnose, Act and Confirm workflow. The proposed North Star is the share of eligible at-risk rupees resolved within seven days, supported by payment conversion, duplicate-payment incidents, hold-resolution time and outlook usability.

Key lesson: A stronger fintech product is not a collection of features. It helps merchants decide while preserving trust and keeping consequential financial decisions under human control.
